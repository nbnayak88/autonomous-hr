## 1. Executive summary

The opening address connected Maruti Suzuki's growth ambition with the wider aspiration of **Viksit Bharat 2047**. The challenge is to build on what the organisation has achieved over roughly 43 years and pursue an ambitious next phase over the coming four to five years.

The leadership message, as captured in the notes, can be summarised as follows:

- Strategy and design matter only when translated into delivery.
- Change management is essential when organisations pursue ambitious transformation.
- Sustainable growth increasingly depends on building organisational capability, not only tracking targets and performance.
- Systems and ways of working are important enablers of consistent execution.
- Technology adoption must strengthen—not undermine—human experience, intuition and judgement.
- HR must help create the right leaders, environment and culture.
- **Culture** was identified as the one-word answer to what matters most for HR.

*These notes capture the attendee's interpretation of the address; they are not a verbatim transcript or an independently verified statement of company strategy.*

## 2. Growth, pace and the Viksit Bharat 2047 ambition

The address highlighted Maruti Suzuki's growth and pace over approximately 43 years, then posed an ambitious question: what would it take to achieve comparable progress in the next four to five years?

### Leadership implications

1. **Translate ambition into outcomes.** Define the outcomes required, the capabilities needed and the evidence that will demonstrate progress.
2. **Treat delivery as the test of design.** A strategy, operating model or transformation blueprint has limited value until implemented and producing its intended outcomes.
3. **Build for pace without losing resilience.** Faster execution needs clear decision rights, capable leaders, effective systems and a culture that supports change.
4. **Connect enterprise growth to national aspiration.** Organisations can examine how productivity, skills, innovation and responsible growth contribute to the broader Viksit Bharat 2047 ambition.

## 3. Four shifts shaping the future of work

### 3.1 Change management is essential

Transformation is not complete when a solution is designed or launched. People must understand the change, be equipped to adopt it and see how it affects their work.

**HR leadership actions**
- Explain the purpose and expected outcomes of change.
- Identify impacted roles, teams and stakeholders.
- Equip leaders to communicate consistently and respond to concerns.
- Measure adoption and realised outcomes—not launch activity alone.

### 3.2 From target and performance focus to capability and growth

The notes capture a shift in emphasis: targets and performance measures alone are no longer sufficient; capability building and growth are taking on greater importance.

This should not be read as making performance irrelevant. Rather, performance outcomes need to be supported by the capabilities, systems and conditions that make them repeatable and sustainable.

**HR leadership actions**
- Identify critical capabilities for the next phase of growth.
- Connect learning, coaching, talent mobility and succession planning to business priorities.
- Evaluate whether people can perform new work, not merely whether they completed training.
- Build systems that help teams learn and improve continuously.

### 3.3 Adopt technology without undermining human experience

Technology adoption is important, but technology should augment human judgement rather than automatically replace it.

**HR leadership actions**
- Design technology around employee and manager needs.
- Preserve appropriate human review, intuition, accountability and decision rights.
- Make automated decisions explainable and provide routes for challenge or escalation.
- Evaluate both operational efficiency and the quality of the human experience.

### 3.4 Strengthen compliance and resilience

Increasing compliance expectations make resilient systems and disciplined execution important.

**HR leadership actions**
- Embed compliance and controls into processes and technology by design.
- Clarify accountability, evidence requirements and escalation paths.
- Test continuity and exception-handling arrangements.
- Balance standardisation with the flexibility needed to respond to disruption.

## 4. Future of HR leadership: the coaching example

An example shared in the notes described **18 verticals, each with two senior leaders, with all 36 leaders assigned coaches**.

The example illustrates how coaching can be used as a structured leadership-development mechanism rather than an isolated intervention.

### Potential design principles

- **Coverage:** establish clear coaching access for each designated leader.
- **Context:** connect coaching objectives to the business challenges of each vertical.
- **Confidentiality:** agree boundaries for coaching conversations and reporting.
- **Application:** translate insights into observable leadership practices.
- **Evaluation:** assess development through agreed goals, feedback and relevant organisational outcomes.

The figure of 18 verticals and 36 leaders is recorded from the attendee's notes and has not been independently verified.

## 5. Culture: HR's defining priority

The one-word answer captured in the notes was **Culture**.

Culture is not only a set of stated values. It is reinforced through the behaviours leaders reward, the decisions systems enable, the practices teams repeat and the experiences employees encounter every day.

HR can influence culture by aligning:

- Leadership expectations and role modelling
- Hiring, onboarding and talent decisions
- Learning, coaching and career development
- Performance and recognition practices
- Organisational processes and decision rights
- Employee experience, inclusion and trust
- Governance, ethics and accountability

### Principles, practice and process

| Element | Leadership question | Practical expression |
|---|---|---|
| **Principles** | What do we stand for? | Values, decision criteria and behavioural expectations |
| **Practice** | How do we behave? | Leadership routines, coaching, collaboration and feedback |
| **Process** | How is work consistently done? | Clear workflows, ownership, controls and enabling systems |

The three elements must reinforce one another. Principles without practice remain aspirational; practice without supportive processes is difficult to sustain; processes without principles can become mechanical.

## 6. Three things HR must understand

### 6.1 Process

Understand how work actually flows, where decisions are made, what causes delays and which controls or hand-offs matter.

### 6.2 Context

Understand the business strategy, operating environment, customer expectations, industry dynamics and constraints surrounding the work.

### 6.3 People and organisational membership

Understand the people doing the work: their roles, capabilities, motivations, lived experiences, concerns and support needs. The same process or change can affect different groups differently.

**Practical synthesis:** HR recommendations become more effective when they connect process, context and people instead of treating them as separate topics.

## 7. Leading a multigenerational workforce

A multigenerational workforce requires leaders to understand differences without relying on stereotypes. Age or generation alone does not reliably explain an individual's preferences, capabilities or ambitions.

### Leadership practices

- Offer appropriate flexibility in how people learn, communicate and contribute.
- Use mentoring and reverse mentoring to enable knowledge exchange in both directions.
- Build mixed-experience teams around complementary capabilities.
- Make expectations and feedback clear while adapting support to individual needs.
- Create fair access to development, mobility and meaningful work.
- Use employee listening and evidence to understand needs rather than assuming them.

The goal is not to manage generations as fixed categories; it is to create conditions in which people with different experiences can contribute and grow together.

## 8. Create the right leaders, environment and culture

The central HR leadership task is to help create three mutually reinforcing conditions:

1. **Right leaders** — capable, accountable leaders who coach, make sound decisions and guide people through change.
2. **Right environment** — clear systems, effective processes, appropriate technology, trust, inclusion and room to learn.
3. **Right culture** — shared principles translated into everyday practices and consistent organisational behaviour.

These conditions should be designed around business outcomes and tested through delivery.

## 9. Applying the insights to an HR transformation or learning lab

The following is an application framework derived from the address, not a claim that it was presented as a formal framework at the event.

| Step | Lab activity | Evidence of delivery |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Define the objective | State the business or workforce outcome to improve | Clear outcome statement and baseline |
| 2. Understand context | Map strategy, stakeholders, constraints and workforce segments | Context and stakeholder map |
| 3. Examine the process | Map the current workflow, decisions, hand-offs and controls | Current-state process and pain points |
| 4. Identify capability gaps | Compare required capabilities with current readiness | Capability-gap assessment |
| 5. Design the change | Define leadership, learning, communication and technology interventions | Target-state design and change plan |
| 6. Protect human experience | Identify decisions requiring human judgement and routes for escalation | Human oversight and experience criteria |
| 7. Embed compliance and resilience | Integrate controls, accountability and exception handling | Control checklist and resilience scenarios |
| 8. Deliver and validate | Pilot, gather feedback, measure adoption and refine | Working demonstration and outcome evidence |
| 9. Sustain culture | Embed desired behaviours in coaching, processes and leadership routines | Ownership, review cadence and reinforcement plan |

### Suggested measures

Choose measures that fit the intervention rather than using a generic scorecard:

- Capability: demonstrated proficiency in critical tasks
- Adoption: sustained use of the new process or technology
- Experience: employee and manager feedback
- Delivery: achievement of agreed business outcomes
- Resilience: recovery, exception-handling and control effectiveness
- Culture: evidence of desired behaviours in routine decisions and practices

## 10. Reflection questions

1. What must the organisation achieve in the next four to five years, and what capabilities will make that possible?
2. Which current performance measures show outcomes, and which capabilities predict sustainable future performance?
3. Where could technology improve work—and where must human judgement remain central?
4. Are compliance and resilience embedded in the system or dependent on individual heroics?
5. How are leaders coached to create the culture the organisation needs?
6. Do HR processes reflect stated principles in employees' everyday experiences?
7. How does the organisation understand its multigenerational workforce without relying on stereotypes?
8. What tangible evidence will prove that the intended change has been delivered?

## Closing takeaway

> **Design for the objective. Build the capability. Enable people. Strengthen the system. Deliver the outcome.**

For HR leadership, culture is not separate from execution. It is expressed through the leaders selected and developed, the environment people work in, and the principles, practices and processes that shape everyday decisions.
