# Autonomous HR Gameboard Model — Curriculum, Missions, Evidence & Growth Loops

**Project:** Autonomous HR  
**Academy:** SuccessLabs Academy  
**Project owner:** Niladri Bihari Nayak  
**Core idea:** Turn the HR learning journey into a gameboard where learners solve realistic workforce problems, build usable HR solutions, demonstrate competence, and improve through evidence and feedback.  
**Mission alignment:** *Architecting Experiences for a Better World.*

---

## 1. Core Positioning

### Autonomous HR Gameboard Model

Help HR professionals, SAP HCM / SuccessFactors practitioners, consultants, architects, students, and transformation leaders progress from learning concepts to designing, validating, and owning HR outcomes.

**Core reframe:** A course delivers information. A gameboard helps a learner make progress and prove capability.

In this model:

- The **player** is the learner and the professional identity they are building.
- The **winning promise** is the measurable HR capability or business result they will demonstrate.
- The **level ladder** makes progression visible.
- **Missions** turn concepts into applied HR work.
- The **reward engine** recognises evidence, judgement, contribution, and mastery.
- The **growth loop** turns completed work into reflection, peer learning, reusable assets, better labs, and new challenges.

This should be more than points and badges added to existing courses. The gameboard is a curriculum design pattern: each activity advances a capability, produces evidence, or improves a solution.

### Positioning line

**“Turn HR learning into missions that build real transformation capability.”**

Alternative messages:
- “Do not just learn HR technology. Architect HR outcomes.”
- “Every lesson should move a real HR problem forward.”
- “From course completion to proof of capability.”
- “Learn the process. Design the solution. Validate the result. Own the outcome.”
- “Build a portfolio of HR transformation work, not just a list of completed courses.”

## 2. The Six Core Concepts

| Gameboard concept | Autonomous HR adaptation | Learner's evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Player Identity Blueprint | Define the current role, target capability, experience, strengths, and gaps | Capability baseline and personal learning mission |
| 2. Winning Promise Formula | State a specific HR problem and the demonstrable result to achieve | Outcome statement, success measures, and acceptance criteria |
| 3. Level Ladder System | Break progression into staged capability and increasingly realistic challenges | Milestone map and level evidence |
| 4. Mission Design Blueprint | Convert concepts into scenarios, decisions, design work, prototypes, tests, and reflections | Blueprint, process map, configuration rationale, test pack, prototype, or case response |
| 5. Reward Engine Formula | Recognise quality, correctness, judgement, contribution, and verified improvement | Rubric score, peer review, portfolio entry, badge, or showcase |
| 6. Growth Loop System | Feed learner outcomes back into peer learning, reusable assets, improved labs, mentoring, and new missions | Case study, lesson learned, reusable asset, or improvement proposal |

### Concept 1 — Player Identity Blueprint

Ask:
- What HR work can the learner perform independently today?
- Which business and technology problems can they diagnose?
- Which decisions can they explain and defend?
- What target role are they working toward: functional consultant, business analyst, product owner, solution architect, integration specialist, QA lead, HR transformation leader, or another role?
- Which experience, industry context, and architecture stream should shape the journey?

**Deliverable:** A role-aware capability profile and a chosen HR transformation challenge.

### Concept 2 — Winning Promise Formula

Avoid promises such as “learn SuccessFactors” or “understand HR transformation.” State a visible result.

Examples:
- Create an Employee Central solution blueprint for a fictional multinational workforce.
- Design a compensation cycle with eligibility, budget controls, governance, test evidence, and an outcome measurement plan.
- Produce a payroll interface risk assessment and reconciliation strategy.
- Build a hire-to-retire process map showing roles, handoffs, controls, data, and employee experience.
- Create an HRIS modernization test strategy with scenarios, traceability, defect severity, and exit criteria.

A well-formed promise identifies **the audience + the problem + the artifact or capability + the acceptance criteria**.

### Concept 3 — Level Ladder System

Use the SuccessLabs Academy progression as the capability ladder:

- **Bronze — Discover:** Awareness, vocabulary, problem framing, and a first guided exercise.
- **Silver — Learn:** Understand the process, concepts, architecture, roles, and standard patterns; create a structured first draft.
- **Gold — Apply:** Solve scenarios, make trade-offs, create artifacts, validate work, and explain decisions.
- **Diamond — Build with AI:** Create or orchestrate a more complete solution, prototype, agent-enabled workflow, evaluation approach, and governance controls.

Progress should be evidence-based. Watching a video can support learning, but it should not by itself prove applied capability. Keep the criteria transparent and accessible to learners with different starting points.

### Concept 4 — Mission Design Blueprint

Design each mission around an authentic HR work situation. A mission contains:
1. **Context:** organisation, workforce, business pressure, and constraints.
2. **Challenge:** a decision or problem to solve.
3. **Action:** the learner's task.
4. **Artifact:** what the learner must produce.
5. **Quality bar:** the rubric and acceptance criteria.
6. **Reflection:** what the learner would change with more evidence or a different constraint.

### Concept 5 — Reward Engine Formula

Recognise meaningful progress, not activity volume alone.

Reward examples:
- **Mission Finisher:** submitted the required artifact against the rubric.
- **Evidence-Based Architect:** justified choices with traceable evidence and explicit trade-offs.
- **Quality Champion:** met defined testing and acceptance criteria.
- **Collaboration Contributor:** gave specific, constructive peer feedback.
- **Impact Storyteller:** documented a credible outcome with baseline, method, and limitations.
- **Lab Innovator:** contributed a reusable improvement that passed review.

A badge is a record of demonstrated criteria, not an unqualified professional certification. Define the rubric before issuing the recognition.

### Concept 6 — Growth Loop System

Use a recurring improvement cycle:

**Mission completed → evidence reviewed → insight captured → asset or case improved → new learner applies it → feedback improves the next version.**

With permission, and only after anonymisation and privacy review, strong work can become sample artifacts, interview scenarios, reusable test cases, case studies, or future missions. Do not include real employee personal data, payroll details, compensation values, customer-confidential information, credentials, or production extracts in public learning artifacts.

## 3. Three-Stage Implementation Journey

The source model uses **Starter → Pro → Star**. For Autonomous HR, retain the spirit of this journey while aligning it with the Academy's existing capability tiers.

### Stage 1 — Explorer: Build the first board

**Indicative timeline:** 7–14 days, adjustable to learner needs  
**Goal:** Select one HR problem and produce a well-framed first artifact.

Activities:
- Complete a capability self-assessment.
- Choose an HR domain and one realistic scenario.
- Define the problem, stakeholders, constraints, and intended result.
- Map the current process and identify the main friction or risk.
- Submit a first-draft artifact and reflection.

**Evidence of progress:** A problem statement, stakeholder map, outcome measure, and first draft.

Suggested tier alignment: **Bronze — Discover**, with deeper guided work continuing into Silver.

### Stage 2 — Practitioner: Run and improve the board

**Indicative timeline:** 30–90 days, depending on the scope  
**Goal:** Turn the first draft into a tested, reviewed solution.

Activities:
- Complete connected missions across process, architecture, implementation, integration, and quality assurance where relevant.
- Use peer and mentor reviews.
- Test key assumptions and edge cases.
- Maintain a decision log and trace requirements to evidence.
- Demonstrate the solution through a walkthrough or case presentation.

**Evidence of progress:** A reviewed solution pack, test evidence, decision rationale, and a revised artifact.

Suggested tier alignment: **Silver — Learn** and **Gold — Apply**.

### Stage 3 — Architect: Build a reusable solution ecosystem

**Indicative timeline:** 3–12 months or an appropriately scoped capstone  
**Goal:** Demonstrate integrated thinking, responsible use of AI, governance, measurable evaluation, and reusability.

Activities:
- Integrate multiple components or architecture concerns.
- Build a prototype, reusable blueprint, or AI-assisted workflow where appropriate.
- Define data, identity, security, privacy, human-review, observability, and evaluation controls.
- Present trade-offs to a simulated or real stakeholder panel.
- Publish an approved case artifact and propose the next improvement.

**Evidence of progress:** An integrated portfolio, architecture rationale, evaluation evidence, risk controls, and a credible value-realisation plan.

Suggested tier alignment: **Gold — Apply** through **Diamond — Build with AI**.

**Journey summary:** Explorer frames the problem. Practitioner validates the solution. Architect integrates, evaluates, and improves the system.

These stages are a proposed curriculum structure; they should not replace the existing Bronze / Silver / Gold / Diamond membership definitions.

## 4. The Eight Learning Formats for Autonomous HR

The screenshot's eight-format model distinguishes learning experiences by the learner's question, purpose, depth, duration, interaction, output, best-fit audience, and what the format is not. Apply those distinctions to HR, SAP SuccessFactors, HR transformation, and HR architecture. The durations below are indicative planning ranges adapted from the reference model.

| Format | Student question | Primary purpose and depth | Duration / cadence | Interaction | Autonomous HR example and student output | Best for | What it is not |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Webinar** | “What is this HR topic and why does it matter?” | Awareness, orientation, quick understanding; introductory to intermediate | 30–60 minutes, usually one session | Low to moderate: Q&A, polls, chat | **HR Transformation Friction Finder:** introduce a fictional hire-to-retire problem, such as repeated onboarding delays or inconsistent employee data. Learners vote on likely friction points and leave with a problem-framing card and next-step resources. | Beginners, stakeholders, or mixed audiences exploring a topic | Not hands-on skill mastery, a solution build, or a multi-week implementation sprint |
| **2. MasterClass** | “How does an expert think and perform in this area?” | Expert-led learning on advanced patterns, frameworks, and decisions | 2–6 hours or a short expert series; for example, 2-hour sessions | Moderate to high: expert explanation, worked examples, critique, Q&A | **HR Solution Architecture MasterClass:** analyse a multinational SuccessFactors Employee Central design, including data ownership, effective dating, integrations, security, local requirements, and trade-offs. Output: an architecture decision checklist and refined understanding of design choices. | Learners with prior knowledge who want expert-level clarity | Not a beginner overview or a long implementation journey |
| **3. Workshop** | “How do I practise this one specific skill?” | Hands-on practice for one focused skill, tool, concept, or process | 1–3 hours, usually one session | High: guided exercises, practice, facilitator feedback | **Employee Central Data Quality Workshop:** use fictional employee records to identify duplicate, missing, inconsistent, or invalid data; define validation rules and propose remediation. Output: completed data-quality exercise, rule set, and exception log. | Learners who need to apply one specific concept immediately | Not broad awareness or multi-week mastery |
| **4. Bootcamp** | “How do I build complete capability step by step?” | Structured skill development across connected concepts, modules, or milestones; intermediate to advanced | Multi-day or multi-week journey with assignments and reviews; around 2 hours/day as one possible cadence | High: curriculum, practice, coaching, reviews, accountability | **SAP SuccessFactors HRIS Modernization Bootcamp:** move from discovery and requirements through solution design, migration considerations, integration dependencies, risk-based QA, cutover, support, and value measurement. Output: a portfolio of connected artifacts and an implementation plan. | Learners committed to systematic capability building | Not a single session or an awareness-only event |
| **5. Hackathon** | “Can I solve a real challenge and prove it with evidence?” | Challenge-based creation, problem-solving, innovation, and demonstration; applied and outcome-driven | Intensive sprint or recurring mission cycle; for example, six missions across 90 days supported by sprints, bootcamps, workshops, and challenges | Very high: teams, mentors, peer review, solution reviews, demos | **Own the HR Outcome Hackathon:** teams select a fictional HR challenge—compensation-cycle controls, onboarding handoffs, HR data quality, or payroll reconciliation—and build a demonstrable solution. Output: prototype, solution blueprint, test evidence, case study, or working artifact. | Learners ready to turn learning into visible execution | Not teaching-first, passive learning, or discussion without a demonstrable outcome |
| **6. Challenge (21 days, gamified)** | “How do I build consistency and prove progress every day?” | Habit-building and repeated application through daily tasks, points, streaks, and milestones | 21 days with daily missions, checkpoints, progress visibility, and rewards | Very high: daily prompts, submissions, peer visibility, accountability, recognition | **21 Days of HR Architecture Practice:** daily micro-missions such as identify one HR stakeholder, sketch a process handoff, explain one data ownership rule, write one test scenario, analyse one fictional defect, or reflect on a design trade-off. Output: a 21-day progress log, micro-artifacts, and a final reflection. | Learners who need discipline, repetition, and visible momentum | Not a one-time event, an expert lecture, or a deep implementation bootcamp |
| **7. Sprint** | “What can I complete quickly in a focused execution window?” | Time-boxed execution to finish one narrowly defined deliverable or milestone | 1–7 days, usually with one clear outcome | High: frequent check-ins, blocker removal, rapid feedback | **SuccessFactors QA Traceability Sprint:** for a defined feature such as a hire or job change, map requirements to risks, test scenarios, expected results, defects, and exit criteria. Output: one completed traceability matrix and review-ready test pack. | Learners who know the basics and need momentum, speed, and completion | Not broad teaching, an entire curriculum, or open-ended exploration |
| **8. SuperClass** | “What is the highest-level way to think about this domain?” | Premium expert-led strategic teaching, mental models, executive framing, and advanced judgment | 60–180 minutes or a short premium series | Moderate: expert lecture, live examples, Q&A, reflection | **Autonomous HR Strategy SuperClass:** examine the shift from HR systems implementation to an interconnected, AI-enabled HR ecosystem. Explore workforce experience, data governance, architecture ownership, responsible AI, operating models, and value realization. Output: strategic framework, executive notes, architecture principles, and decision questions. | Experienced learners seeking strategic clarity before deciding where to go deep | Not a hands-on practice lab, daily accountability program, or build competition |

### Format selection rule

Choose the format based on the behavior and evidence required—not simply the subject name.

- **Webinar:** create awareness and help learners recognise a problem.
- **MasterClass:** expose expert reasoning, patterns, and trade-offs.
- **Workshop:** practise one skill and produce one focused artifact.
- **Bootcamp:** build several connected capabilities through a sequenced journey.
- **Hackathon:** solve a challenge and demonstrate a tangible result.
- **21-day Challenge:** develop consistency through repeated micro-actions.
- **Sprint:** complete one clearly bounded deliverable in a short time window.
- **SuperClass:** build strategic understanding and executive-level judgment.

A format is not a curriculum by itself. Combine formats deliberately when the learner needs to move from understanding to practice and then to proof. Do not assess a webinar attendee as if they completed a bootcamp; align the rubric and expected evidence with the format.

### One HR problem, different learning formats

Use the same fictional challenge—**new hires experience delays and inconsistent handoffs during onboarding**—to show how the learning format changes the expected depth and output:

| Format | How the same challenge is taught | Expected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Webinar | Explain common onboarding friction and invite learners to identify likely causes | One problem-framing card |
| MasterClass | Critique expert approaches to onboarding process, data, system, and ownership design | Decision checklist and design rationale |
| Workshop | Map the current onboarding process and mark handoffs and failure points | Current-state process map |
| Bootcamp | Develop the problem statement, future-state process, requirements, solution design, test plan, and operational handover | Connected implementation portfolio |
| Hackathon | Build and demonstrate a redesigned onboarding workflow or prototype against defined criteria | Prototype, demo, test evidence, and value hypothesis |
| 21-day Challenge | Complete daily micro-missions on stakeholder needs, process steps, data, exceptions, communication, and measurement | Progress log and a set of small artifacts |
| Sprint | Complete one bounded output, such as an onboarding handoff matrix or risk-based test pack | One review-ready deliverable |
| SuperClass | Examine onboarding as an enterprise experience spanning people, process, data, platforms, governance, and value | Strategic principles and executive decision questions |

### How to operationalise the formats

For every HR learning journey, record:
1. **Format and rationale:** why this format fits the learner's starting point and intended behavior.
2. **Prerequisites:** what the learner should already know or be able to do.
3. **Cadence and effort:** session duration, expected independent work, and feedback windows.
4. **Interaction design:** polls, case discussion, guided practice, team collaboration, mentor review, or reflection.
5. **Student output:** the specific artifact or demonstrated behavior expected.
6. **Assessment:** the rubric and acceptance criteria proportionate to the format.
7. **Transfer:** how the learner will apply the method to a new HR scenario.
8. **Safety:** use fictional or approved anonymised HR data; never place identifiable employee, payroll, compensation, health, or other sensitive information into public learning tools.

## 5. The Autonomous HR Gameboard — Example

### Example game: Own the Hire-to-Retire Experience

**Player:** An HR technology professional preparing to own an HR transformation workstream.  
**Challenge:** A fictional multinational organisation has inconsistent employee data, fragmented onboarding handoffs, unclear responsibilities, and poor visibility of process completion.  
**Winning promise:** Produce a coherent, testable future-state design for a defined part of the hire-to-retire journey.

| Mission | Learner action | Evidence / artifact | Example quality measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Discover the friction | Interview simulated stakeholders and review fictional evidence | Problem statement and stakeholder map | Problem is specific and supported by evidence |
| 2. Map the journey | Model current and future-state process | Process map and handoff analysis | Roles, decisions, exceptions, and handoffs are explicit |
| 3. Define the target | Establish capabilities, principles, and solution options | Solution concept and decision log | Choices align with business needs and constraints |
| 4. Design the data | Identify key HR objects, owners, quality rules, and lifecycle events | Data map and quality controls | Ownership, rules, and exceptions are traceable |
| 5. Connect the flow | Define system responsibilities and integration contracts | Interface/context diagram and error-handling scenarios | Failure, retry, reconciliation, and monitoring are considered |
| 6. Validate the design | Derive scenarios and acceptance criteria | Traceability matrix and test pack | Critical requirements have verifiable evidence |
| 7. Prepare operations | Plan support, release readiness, and issue triage | Runbook outline and support model | Ownership, escalation, and recovery are clear |
| 8. Prove value | Define baseline, measures, and review cadence | Value-realisation scorecard | Measures are observable and not overstated |
| 9. Present the case | Explain options, risks, trade-offs, and recommendations | Stakeholder presentation | Reasoning is clear, balanced, and evidence-based |
| 10. Improve and share | Capture feedback and revise the work | Versioned portfolio artifact and lessons learned | Changes reflect feedback and preserve traceability |

Treat this as a reusable scenario template, not a one-size-fits-all HR process. A compensation, payroll, recruiting, learning, or workforce analytics game should use its own domain-specific risks, artifacts, and quality criteria.

## 7. Worked Example — Compensation and Variable Pay

### Game title: Own the Reward Outcome

**Player identity:** HR product owner or functional consultant moving toward solution ownership.  
**Business challenge:** A fictional global organisation needs a controlled compensation and variable-pay cycle aligned to its reward philosophy and business goals.

**Winning promise:** Deliver a reviewable solution blueprint and validation pack that explains how the cycle should work, how exceptions are controlled, and how success will be measured.

| Mission | Strategy, execution, or ownership | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Define the reward outcome | Strategy | Reward principles, business goals, scope, success measures, and non-goals |
| Define programme rules | Strategy + design | Eligibility, budget logic, guideline principles, decision rights, and exception policy |
| Map data and dependencies | Architecture + integration | Data sources, ownership, dependencies, reconciliation, and failure scenarios |
| Design user and approval journeys | Process + experience | Manager, HR, finance, and employee journey; roles and approval checkpoints |
| Build the validation approach | Quality assurance | Positive, negative, boundary, access, calculation, and exception scenarios |
| Plan go-live and support | Operations + ownership | Readiness checklist, support model, escalation, communication, and rollback/contingency criteria |
| Review the outcome | Ownership | Budget variance, approved-versus-calculated differences, data defects, adoption indicators, and improvement actions |

The learning challenge is not simply to describe compensation functionality. It is to make defensible product decisions, coordinate implementation, validate the design, and establish how the organisation will know whether the process is reliable and fit for purpose.

Business outcomes such as retention, performance, fairness, or engagement have multiple influences. The learner should distinguish observed measures from causal claims and avoid claiming that the HR system alone caused a change.

## 9. Suggested Membership and Recognition Design

Use the Academy's existing membership meanings rather than introducing a second competing tier system.

| Membership | Gameboard experience | Recognition evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Bronze — Discover | Orientation, problem framing, short awareness missions, and a starting capability map | First Mission / Problem Framing completion |
| Silver — Learn | Guided learning path, core concepts, structured exercises, and first portfolio artifact | Completed Learning Blueprint |
| Gold — Apply | Scenario-based missions, practical solution artifacts, peer review, and a capstone | Applied Solution / Validated Case |
| Diamond — Build with AI | Integrated solutions or prototypes, evaluation, responsible-AI controls, architecture review, and reusable contributions | Reviewed Build Portfolio / Innovation Showcase |

Keep completion, proficiency, certification, and business impact as separate signals. Completion means the learner submitted the required work; proficiency means the work met an assessment standard; certification depends on an explicitly defined assessment scheme; business impact requires credible post-implementation evidence.

Potential recognition:
- **Mission Finisher:** meets the submission criteria.
- **Solution Practitioner:** meets the artifact rubric.
- **Architecture Reviewer:** gives actionable, evidence-based feedback.
- **Quality Champion:** demonstrates traceability and meaningful test coverage.
- **Research Contributor:** documents a question, method, findings, and limitations.
- **Impact Showcase:** presents approved evidence of a result and its limitations.

Recognition should encourage experimentation and improvement, not unhealthy competition. Do not publicly rank learners on speed alone or reveal confidential individual assessment data.

## 10. Community Code of Honor for Autonomous HR

1. **Ownership** — Make commitments explicit, surface risks early, and follow through.
2. **Curiosity** — Ask for evidence and challenge assumptions respectfully.
3. **Integrity** — Represent findings honestly; distinguish facts, hypotheses, and unknowns.
4. **Quality** — Trace important requirements to decisions, tests, and evidence.
5. **Collaboration** — Share constructive feedback and respect different experience levels.
6. **Stewardship** — Protect employee privacy, confidential information, security, and responsible AI use.

### Accountability partner loop

1. **Commit:** State one artifact or capability to deliver, the quality bar, and the due date.
2. **Check in:** Share evidence, blockers, assumptions, and where help is needed.
3. **Reflect and reset:** Review what worked, what failed, and the next experiment.

A miss is useful data when reported honestly. The objective is better capability and better solutions—not performative activity.

## 11. The 30-Day HR Gameboard Pilot

Start with one domain and one learner persona. Do not gamify all 20 labs at once.

### Week 1 — Frame the board
- Select one scenario, such as Employee Central data quality or compensation-cycle ownership.
- Define learner identity, the winning promise, baseline capability, and acceptance criteria.
- Create a short mission map with 4–6 milestones.
- Prepare the rubric and safe fictional data set.

**Deliverable:** HR Gameboard Canvas.

### Week 2 — Build the missions
- Produce the scenario brief, reference materials, mission instructions, and expected artifacts.
- Add worked examples and common failure modes.
- Define peer-review prompts and the completion criteria for every mission.

**Deliverable:** A pilot mission pack.

### Week 3 — Run a small cohort
- Invite a small group of learners across relevant experience levels.
- Observe confusion, drop-off, time-to-artifact, quality issues, and help requests.
- Collect feedback on task clarity, relevance, workload, and confidence.

**Deliverable:** Pilot evidence and a prioritised issue list.

### Week 4 — Review and improve
- Score submitted artifacts using the published rubric.
- Compare first drafts with final drafts.
- Identify which instructions or prerequisites caused friction.
- Improve the scenario and publish a versioned update.

**Deliverable:** Version 2 of the mission pack and a pilot review.

## 12. Measures That Matter

Measure learner progress and artifact quality first; treat business impact as a later, separate evaluation.

| Dimension | Example measure | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Engagement | Mission start and completion rates | Whether learners begin and finish work |
| Progression | Time between milestones; drop-off point | Where the journey may need redesign |
| Learning | Change between baseline and final rubric score | Whether demonstrated capability improved |
| Artifact quality | Acceptance criteria met; reviewer rework | Whether the work is usable and defensible |
| Transfer | Ability to solve a new scenario without step-by-step prompts | Whether learning generalises beyond the taught case |
| Collaboration | Quality and usefulness of peer feedback | Whether the community strengthens learning |
| Reusability | Number of reviewed, approved artifacts reused | Whether learning produces durable assets |
| Operational outcomes | Relevant HR process measures, where available | Whether a deployed solution is associated with a change in practice |
| Value realization | Baseline, target, observed value, limitations, and review date | Whether claims about business value are evidence-based |

Do not optimise the gameboard for logins, points, streaks, video minutes, or badges alone. These can describe activity but do not prove competence.

## 13. Design Rules for Responsible HR Gamification

- **Use fictional or approved anonymised data.** Never ask learners to upload identifiable employee, salary, payroll, health, or other sensitive HR data into public tools.
- **Separate sandbox work from production.** Learning artifacts are not automatically production-ready.
- **Make evaluation transparent.** Publish rubrics, criteria, reassessment options, and rules for recognition.
- **Use AI with controls.** Where AI is part of a mission, require the learner to examine outputs, verify sources, disclose material limitations, and define human review.
- **Reward judgement, not only speed.** A well-reasoned escalation or a decision to stop unsafe work can be a strong result.
- **Avoid false impact claims.** Distinguish simulation results, learner demonstrations, pilot outcomes, and verified organisational outcomes.
- **Preserve the learner's agency.** Offer choice of role, industry context, and challenge difficulty where feasible.
- **Use accessibility and inclusive design.** Make instructions, artifacts, and assessments understandable and usable across different needs and contexts.

## 14. Reusable HR Gameboard Canvas

Complete this before building a new course or mission.

1. **Player:** Who is the learner, and what capability are they developing?
2. **Starting point:** What can they already do, and what is the gap?
3. **HR problem:** What realistic business or workforce issue will they solve?
4. **Winning promise:** What specific artifact or capability will demonstrate success?
5. **Success measures:** What evidence, threshold, or rubric will be used?
6. **Level ladder:** What milestones build toward independent performance?
7. **Missions:** What will the learner decide, design, build, test, present, and reflect on?
8. **Dependencies:** Which process, data, application, integration, security, AI, infrastructure, industry, or enterprise concerns are relevant?
9. **Rewards:** What meaningful progress or mastery will be recognised?
10. **Feedback loop:** Who reviews the work, and how will the next version improve?
11. **Transfer:** How will the learner apply the method to a different scenario?
12. **Safety:** What privacy, security, accessibility, and AI guardrails apply?

## 15. Recommended Architecture for Autonomous HR Curriculum Design

Treat the curriculum as a connected learning ecosystem with six linked records or entities:

1. **Learner Profile:** role, experience, target capability, chosen industry, architecture interests, and baseline evidence.
2. **Outcome Catalogue:** business problem, intended result, measures, constraints, and acceptance criteria.
3. **Mission Catalogue:** scenario, prerequisites, learner actions, artifacts, rubric, and safety notes.
4. **Capability Map:** relationships between missions, HR domain skills, architecture streams, and the 20 learning labs.
5. **Evidence Portfolio:** submissions, versions, reviewer feedback, test evidence, reflections, and mastery decisions.
6. **Improvement Backlog:** learner friction, recurring errors, missing prerequisites, and candidate improvements to content or mission design.

For an AI-enabled curriculum engine, use these records to recommend the next mission based on the learner's demonstrated gaps and goals—not simply to recommend more content. Keep recommendation rationale visible and let the learner or mentor override it. Track what evidence supported each progression decision.

### Suggested end-to-end loop

**Choose a problem → define the promise → map capability gaps → assign missions → review artifacts → recognise demonstrated mastery → transfer to a new case → improve the curriculum.**

This makes the curriculum more adaptive without making it arbitrary. The learning path can be personalised while core quality and safety criteria remain consistent.

## 16. Recommended Next Steps

1. Pilot one gameboard in a high-value HR domain—Employee Central, Compensation and Variable Pay, or HRIS Quality Assurance are suitable candidates.
2. Build 4–6 missions with concrete artifacts and a transparent scoring rubric.
3. Run the pilot with a small learner cohort and observe actual behavior, not only stated preferences.
4. Establish an evidence portfolio and an improvement backlog.
5. Replicate the pattern across related labs only after the pilot demonstrates that learners can complete the work and reviewers can assess it consistently.
6. Use the same template across your 12 architecture streams and 11 industry choices, adapting the scenario, constraints, and rubric rather than copying identical missions everywhere.

## Core Message to Keep Consistent

**Autonomous HR is not a library of HR content. It is a progression of missions through which people learn to diagnose, design, validate, operate, and improve HR solutions.**

The gameboard supplies the journey; the 20 labs supply the action contexts; the architecture streams supply the perspectives; the sector context supplies realistic constraints; and the portfolio supplies evidence of capability.

The desired outcome is not merely a learner who has completed a course. It is a learner who can explain a problem, produce a defensible solution, validate it, communicate trade-offs, and improve it based on evidence.
